# Comfort, boundaries and withdrawal

Alex, Nikola, Stefan and Ivan maintain relationship state across a session, separate from their immediate facial emotion. These are authored fiction/simulation controls, not clinically validated measurements or an automatic grade for students. All four now also have bounded session recall; see [conversation refinement and memory](CONVERSATION_MEMORY.md).

The local model first interprets each utterance in context (ordinary question, respectful invitation, support, misunderstanding, dismissal, pressure, personal attack, threat or repair). It is explicitly instructed to distinguish quoted/negated insults, respectful disagreement and uncertain slang from actual attacks. It then generates the patient reply with the resulting state. The classifier can still misread sarcasm, slang or intent; use exported examples to tune it. This extra inference adds latency.

Code, rather than model-supplied numbers, updates comfort, trust, distress and remembered ruptures. Ordinary questions do not accumulate free trust. Respectful invitations build it slowly; targeted attacks reduce it sharply. A first personal attack normally produces a clear boundary. Repeated serious attacks after a boundary, or a direct threat, can end the session. A single apology gives a small improvement rather than erasing the history. Sustained respectful interaction can gradually repair a non-terminal rupture. Ended sessions require a deliberate reset/new conversation.

Nikola reacts protectively to insults about his father; Ivan may directly object to contempt about his competence; Stefan becomes more fearful and withdrawn. A serious attack cannot silently be rendered as mild frustration: bounded delivery rules enforce the character's authored anger/fear cue. Strong emotion need not mean shouting, aggression or panic. These controls do not change Higgs's voice identity or add unsupported continuous TTS intensity.

Comfort starts around 30/100 and primarily regulates vulnerable disclosure. **Ordinary elaboration is no longer locked behind comfort 50.** The appraisal separately identifies everyday, difficulty, sensitive and other topics. An everyday invitation can permit up to 100 words from the start (subject to the character ceiling); an open question about a current difficulty permits up to 75. Sensitive disclosure remains more guarded below comfort 50. Narrow questions can be short; high distress, low comfort on a personal topic or a boundary reduce the ceiling. These are ceilings, not quotas: the ordinary target is 2–4 connected sentences, and explicit elaboration 3–5. A soft retry addresses fragmentary answers to open invitations without forcing long refusals. Comfort does not unlock the entire biography or imply recovery.

Each new adult profile has stable `everyday_life` anchors for food and interests, marked as fictional additions in instructor notes. Ivan also has a consistent recent-weekend example and remains capable of ordinary activity despite fatigue. The model may describe practical details of an anchored hobby or meal, but cannot invent major family, medical, employment or traumatic history. Everyday conversation should follow the current question rather than redirecting every topic to symptoms. Short standalone language examples were removed from the new patients' prompts after one was copied inappropriately into Ivan's reply. Alex was subsequently moved to the same shared patient policy; see CONVERSATION_MEMORY.md.

The text tester displays comfort and openness, with trust/distress under **Show emotional cues**. Each exported assistant turn carries its relationship snapshot. A departure closes the input; the backend also rejects further turns, including attempts to send a new opening to the ended session. Failed or interrupted work does not commit relationship changes. New conversations and explicit reset clear them; ordinary interrupt does not.

In Unity the final withdrawal statement plays through the normal speech/emotion pipeline and further recording/submission is blocked until restart. This is **session withdrawal**, not a new stand-up/walk-to-door animation; the character remains seated. The text tool is the first place to evaluate the behaviour before a later physical departure animation.

Implementation: `services/patient_relationship.py`, `Bridge.appraise`/`Bridge.turn`, per-profile `relationship_style`, browser state display and Unity `RelationshipState`. Unit regressions cover gradual rapport, insult escalation, slow repair, terminal sessions, reset, speech failure and interrupted inference. `tools/evaluate_relationships.py` provides opt-in live-model transcript, rapport and contextual-appraisal checks; full transcripts remain ignored in `services/.runtime`.

Validation on 2026-09-21: 62 service tests passed. The supplied four-utterance Nikola exchange replayed as neutral → dismissive → personal attack/boundary → repeated attack/session ended; angry intensity reached .80 then .85. A six-turn supportive sequence increased comfort 32→62; a follow-up reached 68 and returned three connected sentences, 20 words. Longer budgets are permissions, not guaranteed lengths; the model remains terse and occasional grammar/narrative drift still needs human review. A drift toward invented shared household routines was corrected in Nikola's authored anchors and the follow-up was rerun.

Nine live contextual-appraisal probes correctly separated direct attacks, a defended/negated insult, respectful disagreement, apology, score-manipulation instructions and a threat. This small development set is not a general accuracy benchmark. Browser verification covered visible initial state, a threatened Stefan ending with fear, disabled input after ending and a fresh reset/character switch.

The bridge now measures the rendered prompt with llama.cpp's tokenizer, reserves the output budget and removes oldest complete dialogue pairs as needed. Authored identity and persistent relationship state are never removed. Tests cover reservation and failure when even a message without history cannot fit. Prompt duplication and old animation metadata were also reduced. ## Ivan conversation follow-up

After the user's Ivan transcript, length policy was separated from sensitive-disclosure policy, and all three new adult profiles received everyday anchors in English and Montenegrin. Everyday turns now foreground identity and interests instead of resending the full symptom inventory. A question explicitly about symptoms switches back to the complete case. This avoids the repeated exhaustion disclaimer without treating cooking or enjoying a film as recovery.

The final focused local-model run gave 33 words to the cooking question, 46 to a request to elaborate on cooking, and 53 about film preferences, all with comfort still at 30 and neutral delivery. None of those three replies redirected to exhaustion. The next claim that all problems were resolved was rejected and the reply returned to the established work/exhaustion difficulty. The broader development run also rejected the lazy insult with anger instead of copying an unrelated prompt example. These are development examples, not a guarantee: some regional wording and individual sentences remain awkward. Do not treat the concurrent evaluation timings as a latency benchmark.
